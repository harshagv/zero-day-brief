The job in one paragraph

Every person, laptop, and piece of software that touches Susquehanna's trading systems has to prove it's allowed to be there, at every step. This role builds and maintains the systems that make that check happen automatically — who gets access to what, how a laptop is kept safe if it's compromised, and how the cloud infrastructure trading runs on stays configured safely as it changes daily. When it's working well, nobody outside security notices. When it isn't, it either slows everyone down with friction, or it fails silently until something goes wrong.

What I'd actually do, in plain terms

First month

Map out exactly how access flows from a laptop to a live trading system today, and where the checkpoints are — before changing anything.

Second month

Fix the single biggest gap that map turns up, and start learning the parts of the job I'm not yet strong in from people who already own them.

Third month

Take ownership of one area — most likely the access-control side — and be a reliable point of contact for it, not still ramping up.

Where I'm strong, and where I'm honestly still building

Strong, with numbers to back it

  • Designing who-can-access-what systems: cut access-related security incidents by half at a previous bank by tightening how access was granted.
  • Keeping cloud infrastructure configured safely as it changes: improved policy compliance by 38% at a prior firm using automated checks.
  • Building small tools that remove manual security work — one I built cut incident response time from 45 minutes to 12.

Genuinely still building

  • Deep networking knowledge — how data actually moves across a company's internal network, and diagnosing connectivity problems in firewalls and proxies.
  • Hands-on laptop/device security administration — I've been a consumer of these tools, not the person configuring their day-to-day policy.
Why say this plainly: a security hire who overstates their networking depth finds that out under pressure, during an actual incident — which is the worst possible time for either of us to discover it. Naming it now means it's a planned ramp-up, not a surprise later.

A few terms this plan uses

IAM / access controls
The system deciding who or what can do what, on which systems.
Privileged access (PAM)
Extra checks for the highest-risk kind of access — the accounts that can change or delete things at scale.
Endpoint hardening
Locking down laptops and devices so a stolen one is less useful to an attacker.
Cloud security posture
Continuously checking that cloud infrastructure is configured safely, since it changes daily.
Firewalls / proxies
The checkpoints that control what network traffic is allowed in or out.
MFA (multi-factor authentication)
Requiring more than just a password to prove it's really you.
