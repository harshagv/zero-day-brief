01 — Day-one thesis

The first artifact this role needs

Before any new control gets designed, the highest-value first move is a risk-ranked inventory of what's currently unowned across Mable's platform and infrastructure — specifically which systems touch disability and aged-care personal data, which of those lack a named control owner today, and where the highest-consequence exposure actually sits. That inventory is what turns "design controls, lead vulnerability assessment, define standards" from three parallel workstreams competing for the same engineering attention into a single, risk-ordered backlog everyone can see.

03 — Risk-prioritized roadmap

Ranked by consequence, not just elapsed time

Band 1 — Highest current risk

Weeks 1–2
  • Build the risk-ranked exposure inventory described in the day-one thesis: which platform and infrastructure components handle disability/aged-care personal data, which currently lack a named control owner, ranked by consequence.
  • Get a precise read on where Mable stands against the Australian Privacy Act, ISO 27001, and Essential Eight — this is the largest unverified accountability in this assessment (coursework-only Essential Eight exposure, no Privacy Act or ISO 27001 evidence), so it gets the earliest, most deliberate attention rather than the latest.
  • Baseline current CI/CD pipeline security-gate coverage (SAST/SCA/secrets/IaC) using existing gap-scanning tooling already built for this purpose, to establish a real current-state map rather than assume one.

Band 2 — Close a real gap, not a plan for one

Days 30–60
  • Take the single highest-consequence finding from the Band 1 inventory and close it end to end — scope, fix, test, deploy — rather than only documenting it.
  • Formalise threat modelling as a standing SDLC gate (e.g. a STRIDE-based review) for one high-consequence workflow — support-worker/participant identity verification or payment handling — converting a review-practice history into a repeatable, named methodology.
  • Pair on endpoint policy work specifically to build OS-level hardening and EDR platform-administration depth on top of the existing detection-engineering experience (custom CrowdStrike IOAs, Zscaler policy), closing the gap between authoring detections and owning the platform.

Band 3 — Own a domain independently

Days 60–90
  • Take standing ownership of vulnerability assessment & remediation coordination — the area with the deepest verified track record — including its cross-team prioritisation cadence.
  • Launch a security-literacy initiative for engineering squads modelled on the OSL Group Security Champions programme, adapted to Mable's squad structure.
  • Produce a gap-map against Essential Eight, ISO 27001, and the Privacy Act using the Essential Eight coursework grounding as a starting scaffold — explicitly framed as a baseline to build from, not existing expertise being claimed.
