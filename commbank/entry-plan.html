30 / 60 / 90§3
01

Days 1–30 — Read the actual guardrails, don't assume them

Baseline the domain's real CI/CD and threat-model posture
  • Run the coverage-and-STRIDE scan (built and tested — §4) across the squads in my assigned domain; turn assumption into a scored baseline in week two, not week ten.
  • Sit in on existing security design reviews as an observer before I run one — learn the Bank's actual review cadence, templates, and sign-off chain rather than importing a generic process.
  • Map who owns the "reusable pattern" catalog today, if one exists, so guardrail work in phase two extends it instead of forking it.
02

Days 31–60: Close the highest-leverage gate gaps

Turn the baseline into prioritised, shipped guardrails
  • Pick the 2–3 lowest-maturity pipelines from the phase-one scan and close the gaps that cover the most STRIDE ground per engineering-hour — not the easiest wins, the highest-leverage ones.
  • Run my first formally facilitated threat-modelling session on a live service, with the architecture and product owners in the room — this is the practice I flagged as PARTIAL above; phase two is where it becomes real.
  • Draft the first reusable pattern doc (e.g. a secure CI/CD template) so uplift work stops being one-off and starts being scalable, per the JD's own framing.
03

Days 61–90: Prove it scales past one squad

Show the pattern travels, and report it in numbers a non-technical stakeholder can act on
  • Get the reusable pattern adopted by a second squad outside my original one — the actual test of "scale across domains," not just "worked once."
  • Publish a plain-language risk summary of what changed and why (see the executive one-pager methodology, §7 of the full campaign) so the improvement is legible above engineering.
  • Name, honestly, what's still open — including AI-security depth and APRA CPS 234 fluency — with a concrete plan to close it, rather than letting a 90-day report imply the gaps are gone.
