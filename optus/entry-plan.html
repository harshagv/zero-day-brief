01 — Day-one thesis

What gets built first, and why

The first deliverable proposed here is not a dashboard — it is a single, fully worked SLO for one real platform surface, defined end-to-end rather than templated. Standing up SLOs, SLIs, and error budgets across four platforms at once produces four shallow frameworks; standing up one correctly, against real Optus data-flow boundaries once read access exists, produces a pattern the rest can be replicated from. That sequencing — depth before breadth — is the same principle behind the automation-and-toil-reduction work in the evidence below, and it's the right first move here precisely because formal SLO/error-budget practice is this candidate's clearest gap, not a strength to lead with.

↳ re: "Drive platform reliability and performance by defining and monitoring SLOs, SLIs, error budgets, and platform health across core security platforms"
Illustrative — proposed framework, not a claimed past result

A worked example of what that first SLO might look like, using a SIEM log-ingestion pipeline as the stand-in platform surface until the real one is scoped on the ground:

SLI
% of log sources delivering events within their expected ingestion window
Target
99.5% of sources within window, rolling 28 days
Error budget
~3.4 hrs/month of degraded ingestion across the source population
Alert threshold
Budget burn rate flags at 2x and 6x expected pace, not on raw breach alone

03 — Entry plan

First 90 days

01

Days 1–30

Map before proposing anything
  • Build the platform dependency and data-flow map across SIEM, EDR, Vulnerability Management, and Exposure Management — what feeds what, and where the current monitoring blind spots sit.
  • Sit in on the incident and on-call rotation as an observer only — this is the domain with zero verified evidence, so it gets the earliest and most deliberate exposure, not the last.
  • Get read access to existing dashboards, alert configuration, and any capacity or utilisation data already in place, to establish the real current baseline rather than assume one.
02

Days 31–60

Ship one real SLO, not a proposal for one
  • Take the highest-incident-volume platform surface identified in the 30-day map and define its first SLO end-to-end: SLI, target, error budget, burn-rate alerting, named owner.
  • Pair on two live on-call or major-incident rotations specifically to build the stakeholder-communication and incident-coordination fluency the JD requires and the resume currently can't evidence.
  • Extend the alert/ticket automation pattern proven at Mox Bank into an observability-as-code artifact for that same platform surface.
03

Days 61–90

Own one reliability domain independently
  • Take standing ownership of the automation/alerting layer for one platform, including its on-call rotation, where the deepest verified background already sits.
  • Present a capacity-planning proposal for that platform, translating the cost-optimisation discipline evidenced in the Acatus/Pactum Azure migration into a forecast-driven model rather than a one-time saving.
  • Document, plainly, where SLO/error-budget practice and major-incident-command fluency still sit after 90 days — with a concrete closing plan attached, not a vague acknowledgment.

05 — Domain coverage

This JD is a reliability-engineering role wearing a security badge: the object of the work is platform uptime, alert delivery, and capacity — not the security findings those platforms produce. The candidate's background is security engineering with strong automation depth, not SRE practice for infrastructure reliability.
